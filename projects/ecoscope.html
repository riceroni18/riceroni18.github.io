Project Overview

EcoScope is a Python-based biodiversity visualization project exploring approximately a decade of species observations in the Key Largo area.

The project uses biodiversity occurrence records from GBIF to examine where species have been observed and to transform those records into an interactive geographic visualization.

The goal was to make ecological observation data easier to explore while connecting computational analysis with my broader interest in conservation and biodiversity.

The Ecological Question

Biodiversity databases contain large numbers of individual species observations collected across different locations and time periods.

When viewed as rows in a dataset, it can be difficult to understand how those observations relate to the landscape in which they were recorded.

EcoScope explores how geospatial visualization can provide additional context by placing biodiversity observations back onto the geographic environment they represent.

Technical Approach

I used Python to work with biodiversity occurrence data from GBIF and prepare relevant species, location, and observation information for visualization.

Folium was used to translate geographic coordinates into an interactive map, allowing biodiversity records to be explored spatially rather than only through tabular data.

The project provided an opportunity to combine biological data, programmatic data access, and geospatial visualization within a single workflow.

Conservation Context

EcoScope also connects my computational work with my interest in conservation and ecological monitoring.

My previous conservation experience has included wildlife monitoring, vegetation surveys, habitat restoration, GPS-supported field activities, and community-science projects in Southern California.

Working with biodiversity occurrence data provided another perspective on the same broader challenge: transforming individual biological observations into structured information that can support exploration and interpretation.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my experience working with biological data outside of human genomics and introduced additional considerations involved in ecological and geospatial datasets.

I gained practice retrieving and processing biodiversity records, working with geographic coordinates, and building interactive maps with Python and Folium.

It also reinforced my interest in applying computational methods across different areas of biology, including biodiversity informatics and conservation data.
